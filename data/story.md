## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**August 29.** The series now runs through **August 29: 5,966 confirmed cases, 2,864 deaths, 1,346 recovered**. Global cumulative total, suspected plus confirmed, passed 6,000 on the 28th and stands at 6,082. DRC alone is at 5,945 confirmed and 2,862 deaths. The DRC case fatality ratio sits at 48.1 percent.

Three INSP reports landed at once, because the tracker had gone two days without a seatable figure. [Sitrep 104](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n104-mvebdb-26-08-2026/) covers the 26th: 81 new confirmed cases, 42 deaths, 24 discharges. [Sitrep 105](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n105-mvebdb-27-08-2026/) covers the 27th: 69, 38, 25. [Sitrep 106](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n106-mvebdb-28-08-2026/) covers the 28th: 82, 38, 9. Every one of those daily figures reconciles exactly against the cumulative columns, and every per-province table sums to its own national total. [ECDC](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-and-uganda) read Sitrep 104 independently and landed on the same 5,794 and 2,786, with the same 843 in isolation and the same 82.3 percent contact follow-up.

The map moved. On the 26th two new health zones were touched in a single day, Biena and Manguredjipa, both in Nord-Kivu. That takes the national count from 58 to 60 of 151, across six provinces. Two zones in one reporting day is the fastest expansion this tracker has recorded. Ituri now has 28 of its 36 zones affected, Nord-Kivu 15 of 34, Tshopo 7 of 23, Haut-Uélé 6 of 13, Bas-Uélé 3 of 11, Sud-Kivu 1 of 34. Bas-Uélé, which had its first case less than three weeks ago, recorded its second death on the 28th.

The number worth sitting with is 9. That is the discharge count on the 28th, against 24, 30, 15, 18, 25 and 24 on the six reporting days before it, while new confirmed cases came in at 82. Active cases, which is confirmed minus recovered minus confirmed deaths, rose 35 in one day to 1,756. That is the largest single-day rise this month. One low discharge day can be treatment-centre throughput or reporting timing rather than a change in outcomes, so this is a thing to watch on Sitrep 107, not a conclusion. But the arithmetic is what it is. Inflow is steady near 80 a day and outflow just fell by two thirds.

Contact tracing is doing the same oscillation it has done all month. It hit 82.3 percent on the 26th, dragged down by Haut-Uélé at 61.0 and Bas-Uélé at 56.2, with five Haut-Uélé control points not reporting at all because staff were striking over unpaid wages. It recovered to 87.3 percent on the 27th, the first reading above the 85 percent operational threshold this tracker has logged. Then back to 84.4 on the 28th. WHO's stated target is 95 percent. The pattern is that Ituri, the oldest and largest front, holds somewhere near 86 to 90, and the newer provinces in the north-east do not.

Uganda is formally closed. On August 27, WHO and Africa CDC [jointly declared the Uganda outbreak over](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/uganda/news/uganda-ends-ebola-outbreak-following-completion-42-day-countdown), 42 consecutive days after the country's last patient, an imported case, was discharged on July 16. Uganda's health ministry had declared the outbreak over on July 28 on the strength of the local-transmission clock; this closes the separate importation clock WHO had been running since. Final counts are 20 confirmed cases, 15 of them imported from DRC and 5 locally acquired, 18 recoveries and 2 deaths, with more than 800 contacts monitored. Those are the same figures this tracker has carried since July 28, so nothing in the series moved. What changed is the status, not the count.

CDC has not caught up to any of it. All three of its travel notices still read "Page last reviewed: August 04." Uganda is still named in the Level 2 notice a month after its own declaration and two days after WHO's. The Level 3 notice still covers Haut-Uélé and Tshopo only, with no mention of Bas-Uélé, where the outbreak has been since mid-August.
