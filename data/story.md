## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**September 14.** The series reads **September 13: 7,221 confirmed cases, 3,477 deaths, 1,731 recovered**, and 7,337 cumulative including suspected cases. DRC alone stands at 7,200 confirmed and 3,475 deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.3 percent. Active cases, meaning confirmed minus recovered minus confirmed deaths, sit at 2,013. That number crossed 2,000 for the first time in this outbreak. The source is [SitRep 121](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n121-mvebdb-12-09-2026/), reporting September 12 data, with [SitRep 120](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n120-mvebdb-11-09-2026/) filling the day before it.

Two days were missing from the tracker and both are now filled. The reason is worth naming, because it is not the usual one. INSP posted both reports to its site on the morning of September 14, minutes apart, even though the documents themselves carry publication dates of September 12 and 13. The runs on those days found nothing because there was nothing on the site to find. Neither report appears in the site's SitRep listing even now. They came out of the RSS feed.

The pressure has moved to Nord-Kivu, and the clearest way to see it is not the case split. On September 11 Nord-Kivu took 50 of 91 confirmed cases, 54.9 percent, a second straight day holding the majority and its largest single day of the outbreak. On September 12 Ituri took it back, 44 of 87. So the headline share flips around. The laboratory line does not. On September 11 both provinces ran exactly 237 samples, and Nord-Kivu returned 50 positives to Ituri's 33, a positivity of 21.1 percent against 13.9. On September 12 Nord-Kivu ran 157 samples to Ituri's 248 and still came back higher, 23.5 percent against 17.7. Same instrument, same day, different answer. Nord-Kivu is finding more disease per look.

The beds say it with no interpretation needed. Nord-Kivu ran 139.0 percent occupancy on September 11 and 150.0 percent on September 12, an eleventh consecutive day over capacity and a new high for the outbreak, with 342 patients and not one free bed in the treatment centres at Butembo and Katwa. Three of nineteen ambulance calls that day could not be honoured because Katwa had nowhere to put the patient. Forty-nine percent of Nord-Kivu's patients are being managed outside a treatment centre. Ituri, meanwhile, ran 46.9 percent, 476 patients for 1,015 beds. More than 500 empty beds in the epicentre, and an ambulance turned away four hundred kilometres south.

The edges moved too. Bas-Uélé notified a confirmed case in Ganga after more than ten days without one. Tshopo, which had gone quiet, took one case on September 11 and three on September 12, its largest single day, and those three came from three samples tested. Sud Ubangi held at its single case with contact listing around it still under way. None of these are large numbers. They are the numbers that tell you where the next province comes from.

Contact follow-up read 87.5 percent and then 88.0 percent, both above the 85 percent operational threshold. SitRep 121 gives that figure twice and the two copies disagree: the dashboard says 88.0 and the narrative says 91.3. The provincial components settle it. The people seen sum to 24,476, the people to follow sum to 27,816, and the narrative's denominator drops a digit. 88.0 is right. The weak province rotated again, Tshopo recovering from 72.9 percent to 82.0 while Haut-Uélé fell to 70.7 on poor reporting completeness.

Two quality lines worth holding. Community deaths ran 82.1 percent of the total on September 11, the highest share in the recent series, before falling back to 73.7. And at points of entry on September 12, six bodies were intercepted and three passed without a swab document.

WHO's newest weekly reconciliation is still [Situation Report 17](https://www.afro.who.int/health-topics/ebola-disease/outbreak-drc-26), data as of September 6. That is eight days on a weekly cadence, the longest gap since the series began, so the last seven bars on every chart are still drawn as provisional.
