## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 11.** The outbreak passed **2,000 deaths**. DRC stands at **4,449 confirmed cases, 2,061 deaths and 886 recovered**. With Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France that is **4,470 confirmed and 2,063 deaths** globally. The health ministry's own [situation report](https://x.com/Com_mediasRDC) carries the figure and the [BNO News daily graphic](https://x.com/BNOFeed/status/2087394451425472935) matches it exactly, down to the per-country split.

The first thousand deaths took about nine weeks. The second took about three. Julien Harneis, the UN's senior Ebola coordinator, put it as half the deaths arriving in the last twenty days. The crossing happened on the August 10 row, at 2,011 in DRC and 2,013 globally, 88 days after the first case. Case fatality is now **46.3 percent** in DRC, the first time it has been above 46.

Both open dates closed this run, which is worth explaining because the second one took some work. The ministry skipped a bulletin on August 9 and then published two in about a day. Neither wire carrying the first one gave it a date. [AFP](https://www.watson.ch/fr/international/sante/182338507-l-epidemie-d-ebola-franchit-le-cap-des-2000-morts-en-rdc) and [AP](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/11/drc-ebola-death-toll-passes-2000-amid-fastest-growing-outbreak-on-record) both said "Tuesday," which is the day it went out, not the day it covers. [UN News](https://news.un.org/fr/story/2026/08/1159293) put the publication in the night of Monday to Tuesday and named the previous one as August 8. So the date came out of the arithmetic. A cumulative count cannot skip a day, and the step was 87 cases. Two days would have been closer to 170. That makes it the August 9 bulletin, and the one that followed is August 10.

Active cases went flat. Confirmed minus recovered minus deaths, the number moved 1,485 to 1,501 to 1,502. On the last day the outflow was 67 and the inflow was 68. That is the first day since August 2 the line has not climbed. It is not a turn. The inflow slowed to meet an outflow that is still mostly deaths, not recoveries, and one day inside a running bulletin count is noise. The last time a single good day showed up, on August 8, it did not hold.

Contact tracing gave back its gains. It went 83.4, then 84.6, then **82.7 percent**. Three bulletins climbing toward the 85 percent operational threshold, then one moving away from it. Isolation occupancy sits at 716.

And the WHO said on Monday that this probably started in **February**, three months before anyone declared it. Early cases got written down as malaria and typhoid, and the first tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus, which is the common one. Bundibugyo is not. Mohamed Janabi, the WHO's regional director for Africa, said the response is running after the virus and the virus is ahead. That head start is the mechanism behind everything else on this page.

Israel said on Tuesday it was testing a traveller who came back from DRC with a fever. Results were due within a day. Nothing confirmed, and nothing added to the tracker yet.

The IHR Emergency Committee meets on August 18.
