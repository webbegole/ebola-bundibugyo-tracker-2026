## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update

**July 25.** The count jumped this week, and the jump was not the virus moving faster overnight. It was the books catching up to it.

On July 23 the DRC's public health institute reconciled its provincial and national databases in Ituri and North Kivu, the two worst-hit provinces, and folded in more than 300 cases the national tally had not yet absorbed. Confirmed cases rose **369 in a single day**, deaths **236**. Two days later DRC stands at **3,075 confirmed cases and 1,354 deaths** on its own, and the global count is **3,096 confirmed, 1,356 deaths, and 575 recovered**.

So the outbreak crossed 3,000 this week, and it crossed partly by discovering cases that were already there. That is the thing to name. A running count is a measurement, not the disease. When the measurement got reconciled against the provinces, the gap between what had been recorded and what had actually happened showed up all at once, as a step in the line. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/ebola-cases-near-3-000-in-congo-after-provincial-data-review) put it plainly: 2,905 confirmed and 1,269 deaths after the review, up from 2,536 and 1,033 the day before, reflecting the integration of harmonized data in Ituri and North Kivu.

The case fatality ratio moved with it. DRC now sits near **44 percent**, up from 40.7 a week ago, because the reconciliation surfaced proportionally more deaths than cases. More than four in ten confirmed infections end in a recorded death. And WHO's Tedros says the true number of cases could be more than double the official one, because insecurity keeps response teams out of whole villages, so the real denominator is unknown and the real ratio is a guess bounded on one side.

The slope is still the story. The 2013 to 2016 West Africa outbreak took about eight months to reach its first thousand deaths. This one did it in under ten weeks. A public health consultant who helped build Africa CDC told [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/25/ebola-deaths-in-drc-surge-past-1300-as-virus-spreading-like-a-wildfire) the virus is "spreading like a wildfire," and the reason he gave was mechanical: no approved vaccine for Bundibugyo, and an outbreak sitting inside an active conflict zone.

That last part is not abstract. Health workers at the Elikya treatment centre in Bunia went on strike this week over unpaid wages, the same week a doctor and a health worker were among the dead. More than 100 health workers have been infected since May and about 35 have died. You cannot run contact tracing and isolation on a workforce that is not being paid and not being protected, and the strike is downstream of that.

One thing did move forward. Oxford's Vaccine Group dosed the first volunteer in the world's first Bundibugyo vaccine trial on July 24, and DRC's prime minister said she hopes for a vaccine and a treatment "within two to four months." That is a trial starting, not a tool in the field. The response still runs on the old measures: find cases early, isolate, trace contacts, bury safely.

Sitrep 10 was due July 19. It is now a week late. Sitrep 09, data as of July 12, is still the last WHO reconciliation, so every bar after July 12 stays hatched, and the gap between the last reconciled figure and the running count is now close to a thousand cases. Historically that gap closes upward.

Uganda still holds at 20 cases and two deaths, its 42-day countdown running as long as no new imported case appears.
