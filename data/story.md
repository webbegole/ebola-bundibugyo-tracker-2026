## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 12.** WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus told reporters on Wednesday that this outbreak is on track to eclipse the 2014-16 West Africa epidemic, which killed more than 11,000 people out of more than 28,000 cases. "The outbreak had a big head start, still way ahead of us, and we're playing catch-up," he said. Abdirahman Mahamud, WHO's director for health emergency alert and response operations, put the expected peak six months out, and called that the moderate scenario. The longer one runs nine to twelve months ([AP, 12 August](https://abcnews.com/Health/wireStory/congos-ebola-outbreak-track-surpass-deadliest-history-135580129)).

Worth being precise about what that claim is. The tracker's latest row, August 11, stands at **4,470 confirmed cases and 2,063 deaths** globally, counting Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France. That is about a sixth of West Africa's case count and a fifth of its deaths. Tedros is projecting the pace, not the position. West Africa took roughly two years to reach 11,000 deaths. This one has been running since February on WHO's own revised timeline, and went from 1,000 deaths to 2,000 in under a month.

WHO also published [Weekly External Situation Report 13](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-4) this week, with data as of August 9. It is the first reconciled WHO count that covers the 2,000-death crossing. DRC stands at **4,381 confirmed cases, 2,011 deaths and 869 recovered**, case fatality **45.9 percent**. Those three numbers match this tracker's August 10 row exactly, field for field. The row was built from a health ministry bulletin read off a wire, and the gold-standard source reconciled it without moving a digit. Bars through August 9 now render solid instead of hatched.

The week's step was **579 confirmed cases and 304 deaths**, measured against Sitrep 12. The week before that it was 540 and 270. So the weekly count is still climbing, on both lines.

The response figures moved in two directions. Bed occupancy fell to **67.0 percent** against 1,050 beds, down from 72.4 percent a week earlier, and contacts to follow fell from 18,276 to **16,183**. Read one way that is capacity catching up to the caseload. Read another it is surveillance losing people. Sitrep 13 does not say which, and the two are hard to tell apart from outside the response. Health worker infections moved one way only: 140 cases and 43 deaths a week ago, **155 and 45** now.

Ituri is still the epicentre, holding 85.8 percent of cases and 80.6 percent of deaths. Transmission is active in 44 health zones across four provinces, out of 53 affected.
