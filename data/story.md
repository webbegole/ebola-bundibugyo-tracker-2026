## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 23.** The series now runs to **August 23: 5,535 confirmed cases, 2,644 deaths, 1,219 recovered**. DRC alone is at 5,514 confirmed and 2,642 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 47.9 percent. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and have not moved since June.

Two rows landed this run. August 22 was left empty last time because Sitrep 099 had not published. INSP posted 099 and 0100 to its site together on the 23rd, so both dates are now filled from the primary. [Sitrep 099](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n099-mvebdb-21-08-2026/) added 83 confirmed cases and 49 deaths and touched a 57th health zone, Mutwanga in Nord-Kivu. [Sitrep 0100](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n0100-mvebdb-22-08-2026/) added 55.

55 is the lowest single-day confirmed count since August 3. The three days before it were 83, 85 and 82. Hold it loosely. Ituri ran 173 samples on the 22nd against 389 the day before, and its positivity rate went up, 15.9 percent to 20.2 percent. Fewer tests and a higher hit rate is what a testing constraint looks like, not what a receding outbreak looks like. The same day, Ituri's alert volume dropped from 763 to 274. Something moved in the surveillance instrument. Whether anything moved in the virus is not knowable from one day.

The doses are physically in the country. Sitrep 099 records the health minister receiving 16,520 Ervebo doses at N'djili airport on Friday the 21st, of 50,120 expected, funded by Gavi. That is the frontline-worker portion of the [70,000-dose ICG release](https://www.who.int/news/item/20-08-2026-who-and-africa-cdc-welcome-the-allocation-of-ebola-vaccines-to-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo). Nobody knows whether Ervebo protects against Bundibugyo in humans. The Phase 3 trial is the thing that would answer that.

One arithmetic note. The national confirmed total rose 56 on the 22nd against 55 newly confirmed cases. Sitrep 0100 footnotes the difference itself: one retrospective case folded into Haut-Uélé's cumulative count in a data reconciliation, explicitly excluded from the day's new confirmations. Same shape as the Tshopo +1 on the 19th. This tracker seats both on the report date and does not redistribute them.

Contact tracing slipped again, 84.7 percent on the 21st to 83.4 percent on the 22nd. Haut-Uélé is at 77.9 and Bas-Uélé at 53.4. Five Haut-Uélé checkpoints are still shut because their staff are not being paid.

The IHR Emergency Committee met on August 18. Five days on, WHO has published the Director-General's opening remarks and nothing else. The temporary recommendations on the books are still the ones from May 22.
