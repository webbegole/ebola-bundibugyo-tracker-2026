## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds. By October 2 CDC's [situation summary](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/) had settled on a ranking for it: **the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record**, spreading substantially faster than any before it. It passed 1,000 confirmed cases 40 days into the response. The 2018 DRC outbreak took about 235 days to do the same.

On September 27 **Marie-Célestin Karondwa**, an official of the ruling UDPS party, was beaten to death in Butembo after a radio appearance defending the party's position that Ebola is real and dangerous. His belongings were stolen and his house was set on fire. Treatment centres in North Kivu have been attacked and burned through this outbreak and patients have escaped. Hostility to the response is not background noise here. It is part of why the curve has not bent. See [Africanews, September 29](https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/29/congolese-politician-beaten-to-death-after-promoting-ebola-awareness/).

## Latest update

**October 16.** The outbreak has reached an eighth Congolese province, and the news came from health officials rather than from a bulletin.

Two cases are confirmed in **Nord-Ubangi**, which borders the Central African Republic. A health official there described a run of suspect deaths in a mining area in Wapinda health zone, near the Bas-Uele border, with samples sent to the provincial laboratory and to Kinshasa. Two officials gave the account to Reuters on October 15. Congolese authorities have announced none of it. See [Reuters, October 15](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/africa/2026-10-15-congo-ebola-outbreak-spreads-to-province-bordering-central-african-republic/). This tracker does not move its figures on an unannounced count, so the eighth province is recorded here and not in the data files.

Where it lands matters. Wapinda sits on a mining frontier with heavy traffic. Every province added before this one sat east or northeast of the Ituri epicentre. Nord-Ubangi runs the other way, northwest, and it faces a border that the response has not had to hold yet.

**The counts have not moved since October 13.** INSP has published nothing after SitRep 151. The BNO daily graphic stopped on October 14. CDC refreshed this week and ECDC updated on October 15, and both carry figures this tracker already holds: DRC at 9,144 confirmed cases, 4,410 deaths and 2,418 recovered, for a four-country total of **9,167 confirmed cases and 4,413 deaths**. WHO Situation Report 23 is not out. The most recent reconciled WHO figures remain those of [Situation Report 22](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/kenya/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-weekly-external-situation-report), data as of October 11.

Kenya holds at two cases and one death. The second patient was found inside quarantine as a traced contact, not in the community. The 21 day window from the index case's arrival closes on October 24. CDC has still opened no Kenya travel notice, ten days after WHO AFRO's statement on the first case.
