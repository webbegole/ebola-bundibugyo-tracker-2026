## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**September 5.** INSP posted [SitRep 112](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n112-mvebdb-03-09-2026/) and [SitRep 113](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n113-mvebdb-04-09-2026/) on the same day, closing the September 4 gap and adding September 5. The series now reads **September 5: 6,543 confirmed cases, 3,136 deaths, 1,535 recovered**, and 6,659 cumulative including suspected cases. DRC alone stands at 6,522 confirmed and 3,134 deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.1 percent. Active cases, meaning confirmed minus recovered minus confirmed deaths, sit at 1,872.

The epicentre is moving. Nord-Kivu took 31 of the 86 new confirmed cases on September 5, 36.0 percent of the day, its largest single-day share of the outbreak. Ituri took 61.6 percent while holding 80.9 percent of the cumulative. That gap between a province's share of new cases and its share of the total is the thing to watch, because it is the same gap that flagged Ituri in May. Nord-Kivu also picked up a new health zone, Kayna, taking the national count to 61 of 151. And its case fatality rate runs 65.8 percent against Ituri's 45.1 percent, which is less a statement about the virus than about how late in the illness Nord-Kivu is finding people.

The beds say the same thing. Nord-Kivu ran 107.7 percent occupancy on September 5, 237 patients for 220 beds, its fifth straight reporting day oversubscribed. Ituri sat at 50.6 percent. More than half of Nord-Kivu's patients were being cared for in facilities that do not meet the standard, per INSP's own note. Capacity built in one province does not treat patients in another.

Deaths swung hard across the two days, 23 on September 4 and 39 on September 5. The 23 is the lowest single-day figure the tracker has carried, and it arrived on a day of 94 new confirmed cases, which pushed active cases up 51 in twenty-four hours, the largest single-day rise in the live pool this series has recorded. Read the pair together rather than separately. Community deaths held at roughly three quarters of the total on both days, and that ratio is the one that has not moved all outbreak.

Contact follow-up is now above the 85 percent operational threshold for three consecutive days: 86.5, then 88.5, then 86.0 percent. The September 5 dip is not a loss of ground. The number of contacts to follow grew by about a thousand in a day, from 24,497 to 25,500, so the teams saw more people and printed a lower percentage. Bas-Uélé remains the floor at 69.7 percent.

On September 4 in Kinshasa, Health Minister Roger Kamba launched a new 180-day preparedness and response plan for MVE-17, costed at 1.3 billion dollars and coordinated technically by INSP through the COUSP ([INSP](https://insp.cd/la-rdc-lance-un-nouveau-plan-de-riposte-de-180-jours-sous-la-coordination-de-linsp/)). The stated design puts the village at the centre, with rapid intervention teams, surveillance and community engagement as the load-bearing pieces. Six months is a long planning horizon for an outbreak, and naming one is itself a statement about how long the government expects this to run.

Sud-Kivu stirred. After 86 days without a confirmed case, the province reported 8 alerts, 5 validated as suspects, 5 new admissions and 16 patients in isolation, having reported nothing at all the day before. No confirmed case came of it and the province holds at 3 cases. It is a surveillance signal, not a case count, and worth watching for the next few days.
