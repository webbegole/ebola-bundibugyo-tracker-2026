## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**September 10.** The series reads **September 9: 6,864 confirmed cases, 3,312 deaths, 1,630 recovered**, and 6,980 cumulative including suspected cases. DRC alone stands at 6,843 confirmed and 3,310 deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.4 percent. Active cases, meaning confirmed minus recovered minus confirmed deaths, sit at 1,922. Two days were backfilled this run. Neither the September 8 nor the September 9 job fired, and both dates had an INSP report waiting: [SitRep 116](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n116-mvebdb-08-09-2026/) for September 7 data and [SitRep 117](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n117-mvebdb-08-09-2026/) for September 8. September 10 is a gap. SitRep 118 has not published.

WHO published a new [Disease Outbreak News](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON617) today, its first since August 28, with data as of September 7. It matches the tracker's September 8 row on every figure: 6,757 DRC confirmed, 3,267 deaths, 1,590 recovered, 61 health zones, and a global 6,778 confirmed and 3,269 deaths. It also closes the arithmetic from the other end, reporting 963 confirmed cases and 481 deaths added since the previous DON on August 28.

Tshopo started reporting again. Two confirmed cases in Bafwasende health zone on September 8, the province's first since September 3, taking it from 22 to 24. That is the first day since August 26 that any province other than Ituri, Nord-Kivu or Haut-Uélé notified a case. Tshopo is also where the vaccine is. It has 1,582 of 11,703 front-line and at-risk people vaccinated with ERVEBO, 14 percent, against 3,000 doses received of the 11,000 it asked for. Bafwasende's own vaccination site is not activated yet and its treatment centre is still under construction. So the province with the campaign is the province that just started notifying again, and the health zone inside it that notified has neither the vaccine nor the beds.

Nord-Kivu's beds went further past the line. Occupancy ran 115.5 percent on September 7 and 130.5 percent on September 8, 287 patients for 220 beds, the highest reading of the outbreak. Ituri ran 49.5 percent. The spare capacity is still in the wrong province. Nord-Kivu took 27 of the 71 new cases on September 7 and 27 of 86 on September 8, and its case fatality rate sits at 64.9 percent against Ituri's 45.4.

One number moved sharply and it needs reading carefully. Confirmed deaths split 31 community to 10 inside treatment centres on September 7, then 19 to 24 on September 8. Community deaths had been running near three quarters of the total. On September 8 they were 44 percent. Total deaths barely moved across the three most recent days, 51 then 41 then 43, so what changed is the split, not the level. The mechanism is in the SitRep's own table. Ituri's « A ventiler » line, the confirmed treatment-centre deaths not yet assigned to a health zone, went from 387 to 408. Twenty-one of that day's twenty-four intra-CTE deaths arrived as one unallocated batch, and a batch posted on one date can carry more than one date's deaths. Two days is not a trend. WHO's weekly reading of 75 percent community deaths stands until Sitrep 18 publishes.

Surveillance volume jumped and verification did not keep up. Alerts went from 1,508 on September 7 to 2,140 on September 8, up 42 percent. The share verified fell from 82.2 percent to 64.9 percent. All validated suspects were still investigated within the day, 329 then 408, with 154 then 229 transferred to a treatment centre. Contact follow-up held above the 85 percent threshold on both days, 88.3 percent then 86.5 percent, and 88.3 is the highest daily proportion the tracker has recorded.

The money is moving into policy. [Bloomberg reported on September 9](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/imf-weighs-pandemic-preparedness-reforms-for-congo-as-ebola-outbreak-spreads) that the IMF is weighing whether to fold pandemic-preparedness measures into the DRC's Resilience and Sustainability Facility at a future review, worked up with WHO and the World Bank. That is a different kind of instrument than an emergency appeal. It attaches preparedness to a standing lending arrangement rather than to a crisis window that has to be reopened each time. The 180-day response plan launched September 4 is costed at 1.3 billion dollars, and that is the figure the IMF discussion is sized against.

Vaccination in Bas-Uélé is still stopped on the Buta stock-out, now in its sixth day. Ring opening in Ituri fell to 15 of 59 expected, 25.4 percent. Thirty patients there are recorded in critical condition.
