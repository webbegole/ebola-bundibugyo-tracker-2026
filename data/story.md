## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update

**August 8.** DRC crossed 4,000 confirmed cases on August 6 and has kept climbing. The health ministry's most recent count on this tracker, from the August 7 bulletin, is **4,120 confirmed cases, 1,887 deaths and 810 recovered** in DRC. With Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France that is **4,141 confirmed and 1,889 deaths** globally. Three runs did not fire this week, so August 5, 6 and 7 were all filled in this run from the BNO News daily situation graphics, each of which chains onto the row before it exactly on cases, deaths and recoveries. August 8 stays a gap until a figure for it exists.

The case-fatality rate set a new high on each of those three days: 45.3, then 45.6, then 45.8 percent in DRC. It was 44 percent for most of July. A rising CFR in a growing outbreak usually means one of two things, and here it looks like both. Deaths are being found that cases were not, and people are arriving too late to treat.

WHO's [Weekly Situation Report 12](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/uganda/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-5) finally published, with data as of August 2, after two missed weekly slots. It records **540 new confirmed cases and 270 new confirmed deaths in a single week**, 707 active cases in isolation, 18,276 contacts to follow, and 140 health-worker cases with 43 health-worker deaths. It states plainly that this is the largest Bundibugyo outbreak ever recorded and the second largest Ebola outbreak in history, and that the outbreak remains in an expansion phase. Bars on the charts through August 2 now render solid rather than hatched.

Sitrep 12 also did something this tracker has been waiting for without meaning to. Its August 2 figures match this tracker's **August 3** row exactly, on all three fields. That is the fifth independent confirmation that the series sits one calendar day ahead of the official as-of dates. The sixth arrived the same week and is cleaner still: [ECDC](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-and-uganda) says in one sentence that DRC published a situation update on August 6 reporting 4,053 cases "from data up until 5 August," and the BNO graphic carrying those exact numbers is stamped August 6. So the stamp is a publication date and the data underneath it is a day older. The fix is to move every row back one day. It is a structural change and it is waiting on a decision rather than being applied quietly here.

Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, Jean Kaseya and Mohamed Janabi went to Kampala, Bunia and Kinshasa on August 4 and 5 and published [what they found](https://www.who.int/news/item/06-08-2026-africa-cdc-and-who-call-for-urgent--community-led-action-to-contain-ebola-in-the-drc) on August 6. Contact follow-up was running at **75 percent** against an operational target of at least 95. Treatment-centre occupancy in North Kivu had reached **139 percent**. Ituri holds nearly 90 percent of the cases. Tedros: "In some areas of eastern DRC, the Ebola outbreak is outpacing our response."

Those two numbers are the whole problem in miniature. Occupancy above 100 percent means the beds ran out before the cases did, so people who should be isolated are at home. Follow-up at 75 percent means a quarter of the contacts anyone knows about are unwatched, and that is before counting the majority of new cases that were never on a contact list at all. Every one of those is a chain nobody is holding, and each one seeds the next week's bulletin.

WHO's vaccine advisory group met on July 31 and [recommended on August 7](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168100) that **Ervebo** go into a randomised trial here. Ervebo is licensed against Zaire ebolavirus, not Bundibugyo. The case for trying it is animal data: three of four vaccinated primates survived a Bundibugyo challenge against one of four controls, and an unpublished ferret study found complete protection from a research-grade version. Gavi holds 500,000 doses and some are already in the country. The group attached a caution to its own recommendation that is worth reading before anyone calls this a vaccine. Protection against dying is plausible. Protection against catching it and passing it on may not be. A Bundibugyo-specific vaccine is still the preferred answer and three candidates are working toward it.

UNICEF put a different cut on the same totals. More than **300 children have died**. Children are nearly a quarter of confirmed cases and almost a third of deaths. In Ituri, where close to a million people are already displaced by conflict, UNFPA reports maternal deaths have nearly doubled since the outbreak began, roughly six women a week, and use of health services in the hardest-hit areas has fallen by more than 40 percent because people are afraid to walk into a clinic. That last number is the one to watch. The outbreak is now killing people it never infected.

The IHR Emergency Committee meets again on August 18.
