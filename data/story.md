## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 24.** The series still ends at **August 23: 5,535 confirmed cases, 2,644 deaths, 1,219 recovered**. DRC alone is at 5,514 confirmed and 2,642 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 47.9 percent. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and have not moved since June.

No row was added for August 24, and that is worth being explicit about. INSP's last published report is Sitrep 0100, which covers the 22nd. Sitrep 101 has not posted. [Xinhua's Kinshasa dispatch on the 24th](https://french.news.cn/20260824/ec9ee6f054f5466d98196f9e9ea4aca3/c.html) says the same thing from the other direction: the latest official government figure, published Monday, is still the one taken on Saturday. So the newest number in the world on Monday is a number this tracker has already seated. August 24 stays empty rather than getting a guessed value. The number moves when the reporting instrument moves, not when the calendar does.

Xinhua does give the row its first independent corroboration. 5,514 confirmed, 2,642 deaths, 47.9 percent, 1,200 recovered, 57 health zones, six provinces. Every figure matches the INSP primary the row was built from. [UN News](https://news.un.org/fr/story/2026/08/1159352) carries 5,515 the same day, one case higher, with no "as of" attached. One case is not a revision. It is held and logged.

The reason both wires ran the story is that Monday was day 100. WHO marked it with a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that does two things at once. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, above the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 and above Kivu 2018-20. And it lists what the response has built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent on August 18. Mohamed Janabi calls it a defining moment, and Tedros says incremental gains will not be enough.

Both halves are true and that is the whole point. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude on some measures and the curve did not bend. Marie-Roseline Belizaire, WHO's regional emergencies director, puts it plainly to UN News: the gains have not yet stopped transmission in the community.

The number that explains why is in the mortality line. Community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, are about 60 percent of the 260 deaths recorded each week over the past six weeks. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. Every one of them is a burial, a household, a contact list nobody wrote down. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. That is the same gap contact tracing at 84 percent describes, seen from the far end.

Ituri still holds about 85 percent of cases and 79 percent of deaths. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds, and Belizaire says the resources for it are not in hand.
