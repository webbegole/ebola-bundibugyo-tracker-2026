## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 9.** The DRC health ministry's most recent bulletin on this tracker is **4,209 confirmed cases, 1,916 deaths and 828 recovered** in DRC. With Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France that is **4,230 confirmed and 1,918 deaths** globally. It is the first figure this tracker has taken straight from the ministry's own [situation report](https://x.com/Com_mediasRDC/status/2086479475491942733) rather than from a wire relaying it. BNO News, which has carried the daily count since May, has not published a graphic in two days, so the ministry channel is what there is. August 9 and 10 stay gaps until a figure for them exists.

Something changed in the numbers, and it is worth being careful about how much. Deaths rose by 29, the smallest one-day increase since July 20, against 89 new cases, the largest since August 5. So the case-fatality rate fell for the first time in five days, 45.8 to 45.5 percent in DRC. Contact tracing reached **83.4 percent**, up from 77.7 percent on the previous bulletin and 75 percent when the WHO and Africa CDC mission measured it on August 4 and 5. That is the first time it has come within two points of the 85 percent operational threshold.

That is one day of a running count. The active case line, which is confirmed minus recovered minus deaths, still went up, 1,423 to 1,465. A single good day in a series this noisy is a data point, not a turn. What makes it worth writing down is that the contact-tracing number moved in the same direction, and contact tracing is upstream of everything else. If a larger share of new cases is coming from people already on a list, the outbreak is being watched rather than discovered.

One number in the bulletin does not fit. It reports **595 patients in isolation or hospitalised**, against 721 the day before and 694 the day before that. Occupancy fell by 126 on a day when the active pool grew by 42. Bed counts and cumulative case counts are different instruments, so this does not contradict any column here, but a 17 percent drop in occupancy while the outbreak grows has to mean something. Either a batch of discharges landed on one day, or the way beds are counted changed, or beds were lost. None of those is in the source. Flagged and watching.

Africa CDC's Jean Kaseya said at a briefing on August 6 that he and Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus had agreed to [commission a study into whether the virus is mutating](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/alarming-ebola-spread-raises-question-of-viral-mutation/), "because the level of severity of this Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented." That is a decision to look, not a finding. No sequencing result has been published and nothing about it changes a number on this page. It is in here because of what prompted it: this outbreak has grown at roughly eight times the pace of the 2014 West Africa epidemic over the same span, and the people running the response cannot yet account for that from transmission conditions alone.

The rest of what Kaseya said points the other way, toward mechanism rather than mutation. **Sixty-seven percent of last week's deaths happened in communities**, not health facilities, most of them found by testing after death. DRC records about ten contacts per patient. Uganda, which stopped its outbreak at 20 cases, recorded more than forty. Treatment centres in Nord-Kivu ran at 128 to 157 percent of capacity while Ituri's sat at 56 to 69. Around 140 health workers have been infected. A virus that kills people at home, in a system that finds ten of the forty people each of them touched, does not need to have changed to do what this one is doing.

On August 6 DRC stopped a passenger boat about 65 km from Kinshasa after a passenger with Ebola symptoms disembarked and died. [Roughly 200 people were on board](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/congo-intercepts-passenger-boat-near-kinshasa-that-carried-suspected-ebola-case-2026-08-06/) and all were tested. Kinshasa has about 17 million people and no cases. The river is how the country moves, and it runs from the outbreak to the capital.

The IHR Emergency Committee meets again on August 18.
