## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 22.** The series now runs to **August 21: 5,396 confirmed cases, 2,559 deaths, 1,186 recovered**. DRC alone is at 5,375 confirmed and 2,557 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 47.6 percent. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and have not moved since June.

The day added 85 confirmed cases and 41 deaths. That is an ordinary day now. [Sitrep 098](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n098-mvebdb-20-08-2026/) states its own 24-hour counts and all three of them match what this tracker computes by differencing its own rows: 85 new cases, 18 community deaths plus 23 inside treatment centres, 15 patients discharged. No retrospective cases, no province harmonisation, no new health zone. Three for three.

One number moved against the trend. Patients in isolation fell from 837 to 737 in a day, on 85 admissions. That is not the outbreak slowing. Ituri recorded 89 new admissions and 116 discharges, and only 9 of those discharges were recoveries. The rest left the treatment centres another way, or left on their own. Sitrep 098 records saturation at the Nizi, Kilo, Logo and Kigonze centres, 43 patients across five health zones receiving no food support, and continued patient escapes. The isolation census is a bed-capacity measurement as much as an epidemiological one.

The vaccine allocation now has a WHO source behind it. Congolese press reported it on the 20th and this tracker declined to log it then. WHO has since [published it](https://www.who.int/news/item/20-08-2026-who-and-africa-cdc-welcome-the-allocation-of-ebola-vaccines-to-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo): the International Coordinating Group released 70,000 Ervebo doses on August 19, split 20,000 for a Phase 3 trial and 50,000 for frontline and health workers, funded by [$7 million from Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/news/media-room/gavi-commits-additional-funding-ebola-response). WHO says plainly that nobody knows whether Ervebo protects against Bundibugyo in humans. The trial is what would establish that. Compassionate vaccination has already started with 2,000 doses in country, going to Kisangani and Buta.

Underneath the response, people are not being paid. Sitrep 098 lists six checkpoints shut down because their staff are not receiving wages, and community engagement workers in Nord-Kivu and Tshopo who have gone three months without pay. The Guardian reported the same thing this week from the other end. Africa CDC has announced $700 million mobilised and $452 million disbursed. Both things are true at once, and the gap between them is where the response actually breaks.

The IHR Emergency Committee met on August 18. Four days on, WHO has published the Director-General's opening remarks and nothing else. The temporary recommendations on the books are still the ones from May 22.
