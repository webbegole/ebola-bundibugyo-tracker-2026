## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update

**September 20.** SitRep 128 carries data through September 19 and fills the September 20 row. No run fired on the 20th, so this is a backfill. The insp.cd RSS feed listed the report normally this time, which is worth recording after three straight reports that had to be found by probing the PDF path.

DRC stands at 7,672 confirmed and 3,699 confirmed deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.2 percent. The global series reads 7,693 confirmed, 3,701 confirmed deaths and 1,898 recovered. The day added 58 new confirmed, the lowest count since the 59 on September 16, and the five-day run now reads 59, 71, 66, 73, 58. Derived active cases go back up, 2,074 to 2,094. The fall on the 19th lasted one day, and the reason is the same mechanism read backwards. That day's 41 discharges were the largest of the outbreak. This day recorded 15.

The item that moved is contact follow-up. It fell to 79.9 percent, 26,319 contacts seen of 32,958 being followed, down from 87.8 percent and the first reading below the 85 percent operational threshold since September 10. The eight-day run above it is over. INSP attributes the drop to falling reporting completeness across several health zones rather than to a change in the denominator, and the province carrying most of the loss is Ituri, which went from 94.0 percent to 79.2 percent in a day. Haut-Uélé is the weakest at 73.6 percent. What that measures is how many known contacts someone actually laid eyes on. When it falls, the chains that produce next week's cases are the ones going unwatched, so this is a leading number, not a reporting statistic.

Vaccination at the epicentre shows up in the report itself. SitRep 128 records the launch of front-line-worker vaccination at Bunia as the day's principal coordination action, which is the campaign AP described beginning on the 19th. Tshopo crosses 30 percent coverage for the first time at 3,576 of 11,703 targeted. Bas-Uélé reaches 987 vaccinated and has started compassionate vaccination at Poko and Viadana as well as Ganga. The vaccine is still ERVEBO, still aimed at Zaire ebolavirus, and this outbreak is still Bundibugyo. There is no licensed vaccine and no licensed treatment for this virus.

Nord-Kivu occupancy reads 95.6 percent in normed structures, a fifth day under saturation, and 42.0 percent of its inpatients are still held outside a normed structure with no bed at Butembo or Katwa. The province took 14 of the 58 new cases, its lowest count since August 30 and a third consecutive day under 25. Ituri took 38 and holds 77.1 percent of cumulative confirmed. Dungu, the South Sudan border zone added the day before, enters the zone table at 1 case and 1 death, and INSP's conclusions again name cross-border surveillance with South Sudan and faster CTE capacity in Nord-Kivu as the two priorities.

No WHO Weekly Situation Report has published since number 18, data as of September 13. That is eight days, and a second consecutive week the gold-standard weekly has slipped. BNO has not published a graphic for the 20th either, so this row rests on the INSP primary with no independent corroboration. There is no row for September 21. SitRep 129 is not out and no daily source covers the date, so the day is left as a gap rather than interpolated, and it will be backfilled once the report lands.
