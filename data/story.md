## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**August 27.** The series now runs through **August 26: 5,734 confirmed cases, 2,746 deaths, 1,288 recovered**. DRC alone is at 5,713 confirmed and 2,744 deaths, across 58 health zones in six provinces. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and have not moved since June. The DRC case fatality ratio holds at 48.0 percent.

Two INSP reports landed together. [Sitrep 102](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n102-mvebdb-24-08-2026/), covering the 24th, records 72 new confirmed cases, 35 deaths and 30 discharges, and a 58th health zone: Ganga, in Bas-Uélé. That province had its first case less than two weeks ago and now has three of its eleven health zones affected. [Sitrep 103](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n103-mvebdb-25-08-2026/), covering the 25th, records 57 new confirmed cases, 29 deaths and 24 discharges, and no new zone. Every one of those daily figures reconciles exactly against the cumulative columns.

The composition of the 57 is the part worth sitting with. Ituri has been the epicentre for three months and took 84.7 percent of the new cases on the 24th. On the 25th it took 59.6 percent, and Nord-Kivu took 18 of 57. That is one day, and it may be a reporting artifact rather than a shift in where transmission is happening. But Nord-Kivu is the province with the worst case fatality ratio in the outbreak, 68.0 percent against Ituri's 44.9, and its suspect beds are over capacity again at 163 patients for 142 beds. If the weight really is moving north-east, it is moving into the province least able to absorb it.

WHO's regional director, Mohamed Janabi, opened the 76th session of the Regional Committee for Africa this week by asking for a [humanitarian truce of at least six months](https://fr.apanews.net/health/rdc-loms-appelle-a-une-treve-humanitaire-face-a-ebola/). His framing was that the constraint is no longer knowledge or tools. It is reach. Thirty years of armed conflict in the affected zones have displaced more than a million people and left response teams unable to get to communities where the virus is circulating. WHO and Africa CDC put the gap at two to three times current capacity. As of August 20, 158 health workers had been infected and 45 had died.

Read that against the contact-tracing line and it stops being an abstraction. Follow-up fell to 80.9 percent on the 24th, the weakest reading this tracker has recorded, dragged down by Haut-Uélé at 64.3 percent and Bas-Uélé at 56.8. It recovered to 84.6 percent on the 25th, and Haut-Uélé did not report contact data at all that day. Ituri holds around 87 percent. The provinces where the outbreak is newest are the provinces where the tracing is worst, and tracing is the thing that finds a case before it becomes a community death.

[WHO Weekly External Situation Report 15](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-democratic), data as of August 23, published this week with DRC at 5,584 confirmed and 2,680 deaths. That is the figure already sitting on this tracker's August 24 row, to the case. It is the fourth consecutive Sitrep to match exactly, and it is the reason the bars through August 23 now render solid rather than hatched.
