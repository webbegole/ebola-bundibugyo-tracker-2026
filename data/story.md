## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update

**August 5.** No government bulletin covering August 4 or August 5 has published, so the newest count on this tracker is still the DRC health ministry's report for August 3: **3,874 confirmed cases, 1,751 deaths and 749 recovered** in DRC, and with Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France, **3,895 confirmed and 1,753 deaths** globally. The DRC case-fatality rate is 45.1 percent. August 5 stays a gap on this page until a figure for it exists.

That August 3 count is now carried by three independent sources rather than one. It arrived through [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/fr/sante/rdc-le-bilan-de-l%C3%A9pid%C3%A9mie-debola-s%C3%A9l%C3%A8ve-%C3%A0-3-874-cas-confirm%C3%A9s/4019107), and the BNO News daily situation graphic and the [Associated Press](https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/chief-arrives-congo-support-ebola-response-cases-4000-135378371) both landed on it exactly, per country and per field.

The AP copy also settles something this tracker has been chasing for two weeks. It says the figures are "as of Monday," August 3. The tracker seats them on August 4. That is the fourth independent confirmation that the series runs one calendar day ahead of the official as-of dates, and the first time the gap has been visible on a single figure carried by two sources at once. The seating convention came from BNO's embedded graphic date on day one and the whole series is internally consistent with it. Fixing it means moving every row back a day, which is a structural change and is waiting on a decision rather than being applied quietly here.

CDC escalated on August 4. It split its DRC travel notice in two and put **Ituri and Nord-Kivu at [Level 4, Avoid All Travel](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/notices/level4/ebola-drc-provinces)**, its highest tier, with a carve-out only for humanitarian aid and emergency response. Haut-Uele and Tshopo stay at [Level 3](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/notices/level3/ebola-democratic-republic-of-the-congo), the rest of DRC and Uganda at Level 2. This is the first Level 4 of the outbreak. The ceiling had been Level 3 since May 16. Alongside it, all three notices now carry the boarding restriction: anyone who has been in DRC in the previous 21 days, Americans included, cannot board a commercial flight to the United States.

The money moved the same week. The State Department announced an additional **$242 million**, taking total direct US assistance past $512 million and close to doubling what it had committed before ([Ebola Response Update, August 5](https://www.state.gov/state-department-ebola-response-updates/)). Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus is in Kinshasa on his second visit since May and says new cases have doubled in some hotspots in the past week. Africa CDC's Jean Kaseya, back in Bunia, puts the share of new cases arriving from outside any contact list at 60 to 70 percent.

Those two facts sit oddly together and it is worth saying why. A travel notice and a funding line both respond to the size of the outbreak. Neither touches the mechanism driving it, which is that most people who catch this are never on a list anyone is following. Contact follow-up is running around 78 percent. Following 78 percent of the contacts you know about does not help much when two thirds of new cases were never contacts you knew about.

WHO's Weekly Situation Report 12 has still not published. Sitrep 11, data as of July 26, remains the newest reconciled source, so every bar on the charts after July 26 is provisional and every figure above is a running government count. That is the second weekly slot to slip. The IHR Emergency Committee meets again on August 18.
