## Story so far

The outbreak began in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24, and authorities declared the outbreak on May 15. The virus is **Bundibugyo**, an Ebola species with no approved vaccine or specific treatment.

WHO declared a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17, and Africa CDC declared a continental emergency a day later. WHO now says transmission probably began in February. Early cases were logged as malaria and typhoid, and the first lab tests looked for a different Ebola species.

The response has scaled fast. By [day 100](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration), testing had grown from one lab to 19 and treatment beds from under 10 to more than 1,300. The curve has not bent. About 60 percent of deaths happen outside treatment centres, among people the system never reached. CDC calls it the [second-largest Ebola outbreak on record](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/).

Conflict, population movement and hostility to the response make containment harder. Treatment centres have been attacked and burned. In September a local official was [beaten to death](https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/29/congolese-politician-beaten-to-death-after-promoting-ebola-awareness/) in Butembo after saying on the radio that Ebola is real.

The virus has reached eight DRC provinces and four other countries. Uganda's outbreak was declared over on August 27. France's single imported case recovered in July. Kenya and Nigeria still have active cases.

## Latest update

**October 27.** The outbreak total reached **9,995 confirmed cases and 4,845 confirmed deaths** on October 25, the most recent day Congo has reported. Congo added 88 confirmed cases and 38 deaths that day, and recoveries reached 2,689.

Attackers killed a nurse and badly injured another health worker responding to an Ebola alert in Bafwasende, in Tshopo province. WHO condemned the [attack on the responders](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/26/attackers-kill-nurse-injure-health-worker-responding-to-drc-ebola-case) and said violence against health staff is slowing the response.

Nigeria's only patient was [flown to Switzerland](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/1st-confirmed-ebola-patient-in-nigeria-evacuated-to-switzerland) for treatment on October 26. Kenya released its first 20 contacts from quarantine the same day, after 21 days without symptoms.
