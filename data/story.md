## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 18.** WHO published [Weekly External Situation Report 14](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-5) this morning, with data as of August 16. DRC now stands at **5,021 confirmed cases and 2,378 confirmed deaths**. That is the first time the reconciled WHO count has put DRC past five thousand.

The number worth watching is not the total. It is the case fatality ratio, which WHO moved from **45.9 percent to 47.4 percent** in one week. A rising CFR in the middle of an outbreak is not the virus getting worse. It is the surveillance finding people later. When cases are caught early and reach a treatment centre, they land in the denominator alive. When they are found at home, or found because someone died, they land in both columns at once. Three weeks of that and the ratio climbs. WHO's own count of 751 active cases in isolation against 55 affected health zones is the same story from the other side: the isolation capacity is holding, but it is not where the transmission is.

The week between Situation Reports 13 and 14 added **640 confirmed cases and 367 confirmed deaths**. That is roughly 91 cases and 52 deaths a day, which is in line with the two weeks before it.

Globally the tracker now stands at **5,042 confirmed cases and 2,380 deaths**, with 1,080 recovered. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and unchanged.

Those figures sit on the **August 17** row, not August 16. This tracker seats a WHO "as of" figure on the following day, because that is the day it enters the reporting stream. The convention was checked three ways this run and holds exactly: Situation Report 12, Situation Report 13, and Disease Outbreak News 615 each match the tracker row one day after their stated "as of" date, on every field. August 17 had been sitting open since Sunday because no source covered it. Situation Report 14 closed it.

August 18 has no row. BNO News has not published a daily update since August 16, the DRC's National Institute of Public Health is still on SitRep 093 from August 15, and CDC's situation summary has not moved since August 12. Nothing gets interpolated to fill that, so the series simply stops at the 17th until a source publishes.

Africa CDC marked three months of the outbreak on August 17 with a [statement](https://www.globalsecurity.org/security/library/news/2026/08/sec-260817-africacdc01.htm) putting the DRC past 5,000 cases and past 2,320 deaths on the same day, which corroborates the WHO figure from a second direction. It also does two things the case counts do not. It calls for a humanitarian ceasefire and safe access for health workers. And it backs the DRC's submission to the International Coordinating Group for protocol-governed expanded use of ERVEBO in frontline workers, alongside a Phase III trial, while stating plainly that the cross-protection evidence against Bundibugyo is limited and does not establish clinical effectiveness. That is a decision to act on an incomplete answer rather than wait for a complete one.

The IHR Emergency Committee met today. Nothing had been published at the time of this update.
