## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update
**September 12.** The series reads **September 11: 7,043 confirmed cases, 3,400 deaths, 1,690 recovered**, and 7,159 cumulative including suspected cases. DRC alone stands at 7,022 confirmed and 3,398 deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.4 percent. Active cases, meaning confirmed minus recovered minus confirmed deaths, sit at 1,953. The source is [SitRep 119](https://insp.cd/sitrep-n119-mvebdb-10-09-2026/), reporting September 10 data.

The outbreak reached a seventh province, and for the first time it is not in the east. Sud Ubangi notified one confirmed case in the Bulu health zone, a 23-year-old man who left Sud-Kivu on July 10 and died. Sud Ubangi sits on the border with the Central African Republic and the Republic of the Congo, about 1,500 kilometres from Ituri. The route matters more than the case count. [CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/ebola/dr-congo-s-ebola-outbreak-spreads-7th-province-cases-deaths-mount) reports the man travelled through Rwanda and Uganda and across several provinces that had never reported a case, then moved by boat on the Congo River through Mongala, where he spent two days in a health centre before being transferred. Thirty-eight contacts are identified and quarantined. The acting governor named North-Ubangi, Tshopo and Mongala as exposed along the way. So one case is one case, and a river corridor with a two-day health-centre stop in it is a different kind of object.

Watch the denominator when you read the affected-zone figure. INSP now reports 62 of 167 health zones affected, where the day before it reported 61 of 151. The share fell from 40.4 percent to 37.1 percent on the day the outbreak reached a new province. That is because the denominator counts health zones inside affected provinces, so adding Sud Ubangi added its 16 zones to the bottom of the fraction at the same moment it added one to the top. The ratio went down. Nothing contracted.

Nord-Kivu took 43 of the day's 80 confirmed cases. That is 53.8 percent, and it is the first time in this outbreak that any province other than Ituri has taken more than half of a day. Ituri took 30, a 37.5 percent share against 78.9 percent of the cumulative. The laboratory line says what the share alone cannot. Nord-Kivu returned 43 positives from 136 samples, a positivity of 31.6 percent. Ituri returned 30 from 294, a positivity of 10.2 percent. Nord-Kivu is finding roughly three times as many positives per sample on less than half the testing throughput.

The beds say it again, and louder. Nord-Kivu ran 141.4 percent occupancy with 311 patients, the most oversubscribed reading of the outbreak and the ninth straight day over capacity. Ituri ran 43.3 percent, 435 patients for 1,005 beds. There are more than 550 empty beds in the epicentre and none in the province now driving the day's cases. Africa CDC said the same thing from the other direction at a briefing on September 10: cases and deaths are falling in Ituri and rising sharply in Nord-Kivu and Haut-Uélé, with Nord-Kivu infections more than doubling to 381 in recent weeks in an area where armed conflict complicates every part of the response.

Contact follow-up recovered to 87.6 percent, 25,117 contacts seen of 28,672 to follow, back above the 85 percent operational threshold after one day below it. The weak provinces moved the other way. Tshopo fell from 83.5 percent to 72.9 in two days, and INSP flags it as a marked regression. Haut-Uélé sits at 76.1 percent and Bas-Uélé at 77.6. All 429 validated suspect alerts were investigated inside the day and 192 went to a treatment centre.

Forty-nine deaths, 31 in the community and 18 inside treatment centres, so 63.3 percent community. Tshopo reported no new cases and holds at 24. Its vaccination campaign reached 2,006 people of 11,000 targeted, 18.2 percent. Bafwasende's own vaccination site is still not activated and Bas-Uélé's ERVEBO stock-out at Buta is now in its eighth day. Ituri began training vaccinators for a front-line campaign due to start September 15 in Bunia.

September 12 is a gap. SitRep 120 has not published. WHO's newest weekly reconciliation is still [Situation Report 17](https://www.afro.who.int/health-topics/ebola-disease/outbreak-drc-26), data as of September 6, now six days past the weekly cadence, so every bar after that date on the charts is still provisional.
