## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 19.** BNO News resumed its daily situation report after a two-day gap, and the [August 18 graphic](https://x.com/BNOFeed/status/2090292860780199973/photo/1) carries the largest one-day increment this tracker has recorded: **+187 confirmed cases and +98 deaths**. DRC now stands at 5,208 confirmed and 2,476 deaths. Globally the series reads **5,229 confirmed, 2,478 deaths, 1,134 recovered**. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final and unchanged.

Read that +98 carefully. The trailing two weeks ran between 29 and 67 deaths a day. Doubling the run-rate in one day is not usually what transmission looks like. It is usually what a database looks like when it catches up. That already happened once here, on July 23, when a DRC provincial data review dumped 369 cases and 236 deaths onto a single day against a neighbourhood of 80 and 37. Nobody has said that is what this is, so it stays where it was reported, on the 18th, unsmoothed and unannotated. Situation Report 15, expected around August 25 with data as of the 23rd, is what will settle it.

The row also gave the seating convention a clean test. BNO's own deltas back-solve its previous graphic to DRC 5,021 / 1,061 / 2,378, which matches the August 17 row on every field. That row was seated from [Situation Report 14](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-5), data as of August 16. So BNO's day labels sit one day ahead of WHO's "as of" dates, which is exactly the offset this tracker already applies. Two sources built from different reporting streams landed on the same number on the same row. The August 17 row is now double-sourced rather than revised.

August 19 has no row yet. BNO's next graphic is due around midnight UTC. Nothing gets interpolated to fill a gap, so the series stops at the 18th until a source publishes.

CDC refreshed its [situation summary](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/) on August 19 and now calls this the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, spreading substantially faster than any previous one. Its affected-province list still shows five. WHO added Bas-Uele as the sixth on August 18. That one-province lag is worth knowing if you are reading CDC as your primary source.

The IHR Emergency Committee met on August 18. The Director-General's [opening remarks](https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-second-ihr-emergency-committee-meeting-on-bundibugyo-virus-disease-epidemic-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo---18-august-2026) are published. The committee's own statement is not. Until it lands, the temporary recommendations still standing are the ones issued on May 22.
