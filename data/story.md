## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 16.** DRC has now recorded **2,325 deaths**. The 2018-2020 Kivu epidemic, which ran for about two years, ended at 2,287 deaths among confirmed cases and 2,299 counting probable ones. This outbreak passed both figures in a single weekend, three months and one day after it was declared ([BNO News, 16 August](https://bnonews.com/index.php/2026/08/dr-congo-ebola-outbreak-becomes-2nd-deadliest-on-record/); [BBC News Africa](https://x.com/BBCAfrica)).

That crossing is why the description changes. Until now this was the largest Ebola outbreak in DRC history and the second largest on record. It is now also the deadliest DRC has recorded, and the second deadliest anywhere, behind only West Africa 2014-16 and its 11,310 deaths. Nothing in the mechanism changed to produce that. The February head start and the community deaths are still doing the work. What changed is that the running count walked past a benchmark that took the previous outbreak two years to reach.

Globally the tracker now stands at **4,966 confirmed cases and 2,327 deaths**, with 1,059 recovered. DRC carries 4,945 of those cases and 2,325 of the deaths, at a crude case fatality of **47.0 percent**. Uganda's 20 and France's single case are final.

Three days were backfilled this run, August 14 through 16, because no run fired on the 15th or 16th. So three bars appear on the charts at once. The three days added **280 confirmed cases and 141 deaths**, which is a rate of roughly 93 cases and 47 deaths a day, in line with the week before it rather than a break from it.

Two figures point at the same gap. Contact tracing slipped back to **82.6 percent** on the August 14 bulletin, below the 85 percent operational threshold. And Jean Kaseya of Africa CDC put more than **63 percent** of Ebola deaths in communities rather than in treatment centres or hospitals. A death at home means the contacts around it were never on a list. Kaseya's point is that a follow-up rate measured against registered contacts says nothing about the contacts nobody registered, which is the part the percentage cannot show ([Xinhua, 14 August](https://french.news.cn/20260815/a00c1c2dfeda46a68fc7b502d182a8e9/c.html)).

BNO's August 16 tally also carries **306 suspected cases under investigation** in DRC. That figure is not in this tracker's suspected column, which holds a cumulative total of 116 from the June 5 WHO baseline. The two count different things, one a pending-laboratory queue and one a running total, so folding them together would put a 190-case step in the series that no source has published.

WHO has published no Situation Report since number 13, data as of August 9. Situation Reports come out on Tuesdays, so number 14 is due August 18. The IHR Emergency Committee also meets that day, with an updated risk assessment in preparation.
