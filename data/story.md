## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 21.** The tracker now reads its numbers straight off the DRC source. The Institut National de Santé Publique publishes a dated situation report every day at [insp.cd](https://insp.cd/ebola-17eme-epidemie/), and each PDF prints two dates on its cover: the day the data was collected, and the day the report was published. That second date is what this series calls a Report Date. Three sitreps came out of that this run. The series now runs to **August 20: 5,311 confirmed cases, 2,518 deaths, 1,171 recovered**. DRC alone is at 5,290 confirmed and 2,516 deaths, a case fatality ratio of 47.6 percent. Uganda's 20 cases and France's single case are final.

The record that wasn't. This tracker reported on August 19 that the previous day carried +187 confirmed cases and +98 deaths, the largest one-day jump of the outbreak, and asked whether a database had caught up the way one did on July 23. Neither. It was two ordinary days stacked in one row. [Sitrep 095](https://insp.cd/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/95.pdf) gives 84 new cases and 42 deaths on the 18th. [Sitrep 096](https://insp.cd/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/96.pdf) gives 103 and 56 on the 19th. Both sit inside the trailing fortnight's range. The August 18 row has been corrected down to 5,126 confirmed and 2,422 deaths, and the figures that were sitting there have moved to the 19th where they belong.

Here is how the two came apart. BNO News labels its daily graphic with the day the DRC collected the data. This tracker seats a row on the day the DRC published it, one day later. Those two conventions agree perfectly as long as somebody publishes every day, and they had, for weeks. Then BNO went dark for two days and came back with a single catch-up graphic. One label, two days of data, and the offset that had been invisible became a hundred cases wide. So the correction is not really about a wire being wrong. It is about what a date on a chart is claiming to be.

The sitreps also make the series checkable in a way it was not before. Each one states its own 24-hour counts. Difference this tracker's own rows against them and you get nine numbers that have to match: +84, +103 and +82 on cases, +42, +56 and +40 on deaths, +30, +24 and +37 on recoveries. All nine reconcile. The single case of daylight, 82 against 81 newly reported on the 20th, is a Tshopo data harmonisation the sitrep footnotes itself, one retrospective case moving that province from 14 to 15.

Africa CDC's Jean Kaseya put the current rate at 70 to 100 new cases and 50 to 60 deaths a day, said "we have never seen this", and asked for studies into whether something else is going on. His case range brackets what the sitreps show. His death range runs above it. He also announced $700 million mobilised, $452 million of it disbursed. Compassionate Ervebo vaccination has started with 2,000 doses in country, going first to Kisangani and to Buta.

The outbreak reached a 56th health zone on the 19th, Viadana in Bas-Uélé, the province's second case. CDC's [situation summary](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/) has not moved since August 19 and still lists five provinces. Bas-Uélé makes six.

The IHR Emergency Committee met on August 18. Congolese press reports the international emergency was maintained and 70,000 doses of Ervebo allocated. WHO has published the Director-General's [opening remarks](https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-second-ihr-emergency-committee-meeting-on-bundibugyo-virus-disease-epidemic-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo---18-august-2026) and nothing else. Until the committee's own statement lands, the temporary recommendations on the books are still the ones from May 22.

August 21 has no row. Sitrep 098 publishes today. Nothing gets interpolated to fill a gap.
