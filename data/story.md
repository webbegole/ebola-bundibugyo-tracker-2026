## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update

**September 19.** Two reports landed together. SitRep 126 carries data through September 17 and backfills the September 18 row. SitRep 127 carries data through September 18 and is today's. Neither appeared in the insp.cd RSS feed; both were found by probing the PDF path directly, which is now the third time in two weeks the publication channel has failed and the probe has worked.

DRC stands at 7,614 confirmed and 3,676 confirmed deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.3 percent. The global series reads 7,635 confirmed, 3,678 confirmed deaths and 1,883 recovered. Derived active cases fall from 2,079 to 2,074. That is the first daily fall since September 8, and the mechanism is worth naming, because it is not fewer cases. The day added 73 new confirmed, in the same band it has been in all week. What moved is the other side of the ledger. 41 patients were discharged, 30 of them in Ituri, and that is the largest single-day discharge figure of the outbreak. The active line went down because the outflow got bigger, not because the inflow got smaller.

The new health zone is the item to watch. Dungu, in Haut-Uélé, is the first zone added since the count reached 62, and it sits on the South Sudan border. INSP says so in the report itself and asks for reinforced cross-border surveillance. The IHR Emergency Committee named spread to South Sudan as one of its two highest modelled cross-border risks back in August. That risk now has a place name attached to it. South Sudan has reported no case, and nothing in the tracker changes on the basis of exposure alone, but the affected-zone count moves to 63 of 167.

Preventive vaccination reached the epicentre on September 19. Congo began vaccinating health workers in Bunia, targeting the Bunia and Mongbwalu health zones first and about 20,000 front-line workers across Ituri and Nord-Kivu over six to nine months ([AP, 19 September](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/sep/19/congo-begins-ebola-vaccinations-health-workers-outbreak-epicenter/)). The vaccine is ERVEBO, which protects against Zaire ebolavirus. This outbreak is Bundibugyo. It is being given because it worked in past Zaire-virus outbreaks and because trials for a Bundibugyo vaccine are still running. So the campaign is a bet on cross-protection in people who cannot wait for the trial to finish, and it is aimed at the group INSP has been recording as exposed for four months. There is still no licensed vaccine and no licensed treatment for this virus.

The Nord-Kivu bed numbers have not resolved. Occupancy in normed structures reads 98.2 percent, a fourth day under saturation. But 42.9 percent of Nord-Kivu inpatients are still being managed outside a normed structure, and there is still no bed at Butembo or Katwa. Ituri's suspect wings at Nizi, Mambasa, Lolwa, Kilo, Mangala and Mandima are recorded as saturated. Bas-Uélé has no treatment centre and no ambulance at all. The province-level occupancy percentage keeps improving while the thing it is supposed to measure does not move.

Nord-Kivu took 18 of the 73 new cases, a second consecutive day under 25 after the 43-to-50 run of the previous week. Ituri took 46 and holds 77.2 percent of cumulative confirmed. Contact follow-up sits at 87.8 percent, an eighth consecutive day above the 85 percent threshold, with Sud Ubangi at 100 percent on a tiny denominator of 42 contacts and Tshopo the weakest at 75.3 percent. Sud Ubangi sent samples from two community deaths to the INRB and the results are pending, the first movement on that province since its index case on September 11.
