## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update

**August 2.** BNO's August 2 situation report, posted midday August 3, puts DRC at **3,748 confirmed cases, 1,657 deaths and 708 recovered**. With Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France that is **3,769 confirmed and 1,659 deaths** globally. Seventy-four cases and thirty-six deaths on the day. August 3 stays a gap on this page until a figure for that date exists.

The active-case line fell. Not much. It went from 1,387 to 1,383, a drop of four. But it is the first fall since July 13, and the mechanism behind it is worth walking through, because active is the only number on this tracker that can go down.

Active is confirmed cases minus recovered minus dead. Everything else here is cumulative, so it only climbs. Active is a stock, not a running total: cases flow in when someone is newly confirmed, and out when they either recover or die. On August 2 the inflow was seventy-four. The outflow was forty-two recoveries plus thirty-six deaths, seventy-eight. Outflow beat inflow by four.

Recoveries running ahead of deaths is not new, and I want to be careful about that, because it reads like a milestone and it is not one. It has happened on ten days of this outbreak, most recently July 13. What is different is the size. Forty-two is the second-largest single-day recovery figure in the series, behind only the plus-101 on July 19, and that one was a catch-up after BNO went four days without publishing rather than a real day's discharges. Forty-two appears to be a real day.

A drop of four on a base of 1,383 is inside the noise of a running wire count. It is not a turn. What it does say is that the response is now clearing patients about as fast as Ituri is producing them, with no margin on either side.

Where that margin would come from is being built right now. [UN News reported August 3](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168064) that health partners open the country's largest Ebola treatment centre this week, at a hundred beds, and that a transit centre has opened at the Kigonze displacement camp on the edge of Bunia. The transit centre is the more interesting of the two structurally. It sits upstream of treatment. Its job is to catch a suspected case at the camp and route it, rather than wait for that person to present at a hospital already infectious. Around 270,000 people displaced by the fighting live in sites across Ituri, which is the reason the upstream end is where the beds matter.

The clinical picture is also widening. Aid teams report the disease appears to be causing stillbirths and premature deliveries. That is a category of harm that does not appear in any column on this tracker. Confirmed, recovered, dead. A pregnancy lost to Bundibugyo is counted, if at all, under the mother's row.

WHO's Weekly Situation Report 12 has not published. Sitrep 11, data as of July 26, is still the newest reconciled source, which means every bar on the charts after July 26 is provisional and every figure above is a running wire count. That is the second weekly slot to slip.

The IHR Emergency Committee meets again on August 18.
