## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

At day 100, on August 24, WHO published a [statement](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/news/over-5200-cases-recorded-democratic-republic-congocrosses100-days-ebola-outbreak-declaration) that sets the frame for everything since. It calls this the DRC's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak, averaging around 90 confirmed cases a day over three months, faster than the equivalent stretch of West Africa 2014-16 or Kivu 2018-20. It also lists what the response built in the same window. Testing went from one site to 19 labs and 3,000 samples a day. Treatment capacity went from under 10 beds to more than 1,300. Contact follow-up went from 9 percent in the first week to 84 percent by mid-August. Both halves are true at once, and that is the thing to hold onto. The response scaled by two orders of magnitude and the curve did not bend. The number that explains why sits in the mortality line: community deaths, the ones happening outside a treatment centre, run at about 60 percent of the roughly 260 deaths recorded each week. A death outside a treatment centre is a case the system never got its hands on. You can build 1,300 beds and still lose, because beds only work on people who reach them. WHO's stated targets from here are contact tracing at 95 percent and capacity at 3,000 beds.

## Latest update

**September 22.** Two reports landed at once. SitRep 129 carries data through September 20 and fills the September 21 row; SitRep 130 carries data through September 21 and fills September 22. Neither showed up in the insp.cd RSS feed or the SitRep listing page. Both had to be found by probing the PDF path directly, which is now the fourth time this month, so the probe is the discovery method and the feed is the fallback.

DRC stands at 7,773 confirmed and 3,759 confirmed deaths, a crude fatality rate of 48.4 percent. The global series reads 7,794 confirmed, 3,761 confirmed deaths and 1,954 recovered. The two days added 61 and then 40 new confirmed. That 40 is the lowest single-day count since early August, and it sits well under the five days before it, which read 71, 66, 73, 58 and 61. Derived active cases go 2,094, then 2,099, then 2,079.

[WHO Weekly External Situation Report 19](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-4) also published, data as of September 20, after a nine-day gap that was the longest of the outbreak. It reports DRC at 7,733 confirmed and 3,732 deaths, which is an exact match on this tracker's September 21 row, the row that carries September 20 data. BNO's daily graphic, posted the same day, matches the same row at the global level. So for the first time the daily primary, the weekly gold standard and the wire all agree to the case.

Ituri's share of new cases fell below half. It took 19 of the 40, or 47.5 percent, while still holding 76.8 percent of the cumulative count. That gap between share-of-new and share-of-cumulative is the thing to watch. It means the epicentre is no longer where most of this week's transmission is happening, and the outbreak's centre of gravity is drifting toward Nord-Kivu and the smaller provinces.

Nord-Kivu came off saturation. The Matanda treatment centre in the Katwa health zone opened, which took provincial bed capacity in normed structures from 228 to 308. Occupancy dropped to 66.2 percent from 91.2, and the share of inpatients being managed outside a normed structure fell to 18.9 percent from 42.0. That is the first real relief since the province went under saturation on September 15. The challenges section still records no bed available at Butembo or Katwa, so the pressure moved rather than disappeared.

Contact follow-up recovered two days running, 79.9 to 81.4 to 84.5 percent, and is still under the 85 percent operational threshold. Ituri came back to 85.5 percent from 79.2. No province followed all of its contacts. What that number measures is how many known contacts someone actually laid eyes on, so when it sags the chains that produce next week's cases are the ones going unwatched.

Laboratory positivity for September 21 was 10.4 percent, 40 positives out of 384 samples, the lowest the tracker has recorded. Nord-Kivu ran 7.2 percent on 180 samples. Tshopo shows 75 percent but on four samples, which is a denominator, not a signal.

Vaccination at Tshopo reached 3,774 front-line and first-line workers against a 3,549 pre-registration list, 32 percent of the 11,703 target, with 226 thawed doses expiring on September 24. The vaccine is ERVEBO, aimed at Zaire ebolavirus, and this outbreak is Bundibugyo. There is no licensed vaccine and no licensed treatment for this virus.
