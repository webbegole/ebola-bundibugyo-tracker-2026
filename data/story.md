## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

WHO said on August 10 that transmission probably began in **February**, three months before the declaration. Early cases were recorded as malaria and typhoid, and the first laboratory tests looked for Zaire ebolavirus rather than Bundibugyo. Mohamed Janabi, WHO's regional director for Africa, described the response as running behind the virus. That head start is the mechanism behind most of what follows.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update
**August 13.** A man travelled out of the containment zone in Haut-Uele, reached Buta, and died there. Buta is the capital of Bas-Uele, a province that had recorded nothing until this week. Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya announced the death on Thursday. The outbreak now sits in **six provinces** and 54 health zones, after holding at five since June ([Al Jazeera, 13 August](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/dr-congo-ebola-outbreak-spreads-to-a-sixth-province)).

That is one death, and it is worth saying plainly why one death is the headline. Bas-Uele is not adjacent spread. It is a person moving. Every province added so far has been added the same way, by travel rather than by a widening front, which is what makes containment a question about roads and rivers as much as about beds. Kaseya's own framing: "if we do not stop this outbreak, it will last more than a year and will be the largest in the world."

The tracker's latest row, August 13, stands at **4,686 confirmed cases and 2,186 deaths** globally, with 984 recovered. DRC carries 4,665 of those cases and 2,184 of the deaths, at a crude case fatality of **46.8 percent**, an outbreak high. Uganda's 20 and France's single case are final.

DRC crossed a marker in its own history on this bulletin. At 4,665 confirmed cases this is now the **largest Ebola outbreak the country has recorded**, past the 2018-2020 Kivu epidemic that ended at 3,481 cases and 2,299 deaths. On deaths it is 115 short. Kivu took about two years. This one was declared on May 15.

One number moved the other way. Derived active cases fell for the first time since August 3, from 1,520 to **1,516**. The fall is four cases wide. The day's outflow was 47 recoveries and 56 deaths against 99 new confirmed cases, so the active pool shrank because more people died than were counted in, not because the outbreak is resolving. The same one-day fall showed up on August 8 and did not hold. Contact tracing recovered to **84.2 percent** from 82.7, still short of the 85 percent operational threshold.

Two days were backfilled this run, August 12 and August 13. The August 12 step was the largest of the outbreak outside the July 23 database reconciliation: **117 confirmed cases and 67 deaths** in a single bulletin.

The suspected case in Israel flagged on August 11 tested negative. Israel's Health Ministry has ruled out Ebola.

WHO has published no Situation Report since number 13, data as of August 9. The IHR Emergency Committee meets on August 18.
