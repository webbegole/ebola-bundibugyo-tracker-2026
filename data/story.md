## Story so far

The 2026 Ebola outbreak began in early May in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Ituri Province. The earliest known symptom onset is April 24; authorities formally declared the outbreak on May 15. Laboratory analysis at the Institut National de Recherche Biomedicale identified the pathogen as **Bundibugyo virus (BDBV)**, one of four orthoebolavirus species that cause Ebola disease in humans. Unlike the more common Zaire ebolavirus, Bundibugyo currently has no approved vaccine or specific therapeutic.

The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a [Public Health Emergency of International Concern](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern) on May 17. Africa CDC declared a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security on May 18. Within the first ten days, cases spread across 12 health zones in three DRC provinces (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu) plus imported and locally-transmitted cases in Kampala, Uganda.

The response is operating under acute constraints: armed conflict in the affected zones, community distrust of containment measures (multiple Ebola treatment centers have been attacked, with patients escaping), high population mobility, and the absence of a Bundibugyo-specific vaccine. Healthcare-associated infections in Uganda raise concerns about undetected transmission chains in urban centers.

## Latest update

**August 4.** The DRC health ministry's situation report for August 3, released Wednesday, puts DRC at **3,874 confirmed cases, 1,751 deaths and 749 recovered** ([Anadolu, August 5](https://www.aa.com.tr/fr/sante/rdc-le-bilan-de-l%C3%A9pid%C3%A9mie-debola-s%C3%A9l%C3%A8ve-%C3%A0-3-874-cas-confirm%C3%A9s/4019107)). With Uganda's final 20 and the single case in France that is **3,895 confirmed and 1,753 deaths** globally. The case-fatality rate crossed 45 percent for the first time, at 45.1.

Two days went in this run, August 3 and August 4, because the scheduled run for the 4th did not fire. Filling them turned into a question about what date a number belongs to, and the answer came from an odd place.

The Associated Press [reported](https://abcnews.com/Health/wireStory/ebola-kills-1700-eastern-congo-fastest-growing-outbreak-135346370) 3,802 cases and 1,707 deaths on August 4, and said the figure was "as of Tuesday," which was August 4 itself. That could not be right. A government bulletin does not report the same day it covers, and the ministry's own report for August 3 already stood higher at 3,874. So the AP number had to be an older snapshot, but which one?

The ministry answered it without meaning to. Its August 3 bulletin notes 22 new recoveries in the previous 24 hours. Recovered stood at 727 in the AP figure and 749 in the ministry's. 727 plus 22 is 749. So the AP number is the August 2 count, one day behind what the wire said it was, and it seats on August 3 under this tracker's convention of as-of plus one.

I am walking through that because it is the sort of thing that gets buried. A wire service attaches a date to a number, the number goes into a chart, and nobody checks whether the date survives contact with the arithmetic. Here it did not. The recovery delta was the only field in the bulletin that could settle it, and it settled it exactly.

On the substance: active cases fell 15 on August 3, the largest one-day fall of the outbreak, then rose 6 on August 4. That is two falls in three days after three weeks of unbroken growth. It is not yet a turn. The August 3 fall sits on a bulletin with an unusually heavy death count (+50) as well as recoveries (+19), and a stock that shrinks because people are dying is not the same as a stock that shrinks because the response is working.

The response is scaling, and the people running it are saying plainly that it is not scaling fast enough. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus arrived in Kinshasa Wednesday and [wrote](https://www.aa.com.tr/fr/sante/le-chef-de-l-oms-appelle-%C3%A0-intensifier-d-urgence-la-riposte-contre-ebola-en-rdc/4019564) that new cases have doubled in some hotspots in the past week alone. WHO has stood up a mobile laboratory at the Kasenyi crossing on Lake Albert, a transit point into Uganda serving more than 94,000 people, so that a case can be confirmed at the border instead of somewhere past it. Africa CDC's Jean Kaseya, on his second visit to Bunia on August 4, said nearly 80 percent of new cases are not coming off any contact list. Contact follow-up is running at 78.4 percent.

That last pair of numbers is the whole problem in miniature. Following 78 percent of the contacts you know about does not help much when 80 percent of new cases were never on the list.

WHO's Weekly Situation Report 12 has still not published. Sitrep 11, data as of July 26, remains the newest reconciled source, so every bar on the charts after July 26 is provisional and every figure above is a running government count. That is the second weekly slot to slip.

August 5 stays a gap on this page until a figure for that date exists. The IHR Emergency Committee meets again on August 18.
